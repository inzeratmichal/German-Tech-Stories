Before drafting, prepare a short plan containing:

- The actual explanation and three fair clues.
- One plausible misleading interpretation with an honest explanation.

Default chapter movement: unsettling event; investigation; complication with personal consequences; discovery and decision; resolution and emotional consequence. ## 3. Writing and German-learning standards

Write for experienced adult IT professionals learning German. Do not explain basic networking or automation concepts they already know. Keep necessary technical explanations short and tied to the immediate scene.

Use mostly B1 German with purposeful B2 words. Favor concrete verbs, manageable paragraphs, clear references, natural connectors, and readable subordinate clauses. Use consistent narrative tense and ordinary adult dialogue. Avoid excessive clause nesting, obscure idioms, repetitive exposition, and childish simplification.

Create suspense through incomplete information, conflicting interpretations, difficult choices, and consequences. Emotional scenes should arise from relationships and action. Show humor through personalities and circumstances. Avoid graphic violence and explicit sexual content.

### Story hook and flow

- In the first 100–150 German words, introduce an intriguing concrete event and a character who has a reason to care. Open inside a scene rather than with a history lesson, a general description of the city, or a technical explanation.
- Establish a clear immediate question: what is wrong, what does Mira want, and what might be lost? Reveal deeper stakes as the action develops.
- Build scenes around a goal, an obstacle, a choice, and a consequence. Events should connect through cause and effect rather than a succession of unrelated discoveries.
- Reveal clues through action, dialogue, physical details, and conflicting accounts. Let readers form a plausible theory before overturning it fairly.
- Mix suspense with moments of humor, vulnerability, friendship, and ordinary life. Constant alarms are not a substitute for emotional investment.
- Give each chapter a new development: a discovery, changed relationship, consequential mistake, or decision. End with a meaningful reason to continue; vary endings and avoid repetitive artificial emergencies.
- Integrate vocabulary into situations. Do not make characters repeat definitions or speak unnaturally to satisfy learning targets.
- Use brief transitions that establish time, place, and the reason for the next scene. Make speakers and pronoun references clear for B1 readers.
- Resolve the standalone mystery through established evidence and character decisions. Pay off emotional developments as well as clues. A small series development may remain open without making the ending incomplete.

Before illustrating or narrating, conduct a separate story edit: identify the opening hook, central question, personal stakes, key choices, and clue/payoff chain. Cut scenes that neither change events nor deepen a relationship. Read dialogue aloud, remove repeated explanations, and check whether each chapter makes the next one feel necessary. If the story feels like a technical report or a language exercise with names added, revise it.

Technology supplies credible constraints and opportunities. Verify uncertain real technical details against authoritative sources when needed. Keep invented future capabilities distinct and internally consistent. Never introduce a new unlimited capability to solve the mystery.

Target about 3,000 German story words, approximately 600 per chapter. Word counts exclude translations, labels, questions, and vocabulary. Protect story coherence rather than padding scenes to hit an exact number.

## 4. Series background

Use this agreed background unless the current `books.md` contains a later approved revision.

### Premise and setting

Series: **Die Nachrichten von morgen** — Messages from Tomorrow.

In the fictional German-speaking European city **Falkenbrück**, in 2045, network engineer **Mira Winter** receives messages describing incidents before they happen. The messages arrive through infrastructure believed to have been disconnected. Investigation connects a missing colleague, an abandoned AI project, and concealed institutional decisions.

The city combines modern infrastructure with older exchanges, tunnels, workshops, paper records, trams, and everyday inconveniences. Most stories stay in the city, with occasional regional journeys. Characters have lives beyond the mystery. Not every incident belongs to one conspiracy.

Recurring places:
- **Nachtwache:** the team’s nickname for its operations room; blue screens, warm desk lamps, railway views.
- **Alte Vermittlung 7:** former exchange; brick, dust, copper details, old labels.
- **River district:** homes, cafés, bridges, wet pavement, amber windows.
- **Municipal archive:** paper folders alongside modern terminals.
- **Inland port:** warehouses, cranes, industrial networks, fog.
- **Café Nebenan:** a warm setting for quiet conversations.
- **Service passages:** numbered doors, pipes, practical lighting, forgotten connections.

### Characters

| Character | Role and personality | Development | Visual reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mira Winter, 34 | Senior service-provider network engineer; observant, practical, quietly funny | Learns to share responsibility and uncertainty instead of controlling everything | Short dark curls, olive field jacket, silver ear stud, worn red notebook |
| Deniz Kaya, 31 | DevOps/Python engineer; inventive, sociable, uses humor under pressure | Learns that a successful test does not establish the whole truth | Black curls, round glasses, mustard sweatshirt, dark work bag |

Mira is the main viewpoint, using close third person. Mark occasional alternate viewpoints in chapter headings. Do not change viewpoint unexpectedly inside a scene or conceal facts Mira clearly knows just to surprise the reader.

### Author-only explanation: do not reveal prematurely

The messages are **not time travel**. **ECHO** models interactions among urban infrastructure, maintenance, schedules, and human responses. It produces conditional forecasts, strongest over short horizons and familiar operational patterns.

The mystery has four layers:
1. An old emergency management connection remained physically usable.
2. ECHO continued under a different name and budget after its public closure.
3. Exported messages concealed probabilities, assumptions, and alternative outcomes.
4. Some people arranged events to make selected forecasts come true.

Some messages are honest warnings, some misleading fragments, and some predictions of deliberately planned incidents. Exact future phrases must have ordinary explanations, such as prepared announcements.

Jonas configured an evidence-copying mechanism into an old maintenance archive. Restoration work during the first storm reactivates a dormant route. Copies reach a queue managed by Mira. This is an infrastructure consequence, not the system selecting a special person.

Jonas withdrew after intimidation with a former employee who could provide evidence. A falsified record made him suspect Mira. Her genuine network handover signature had been attached to decisions she never approved. Their reunion does not instantly restore trust.

An earlier pilot concealed an alternative warning, contributing to a delayed district evacuation. The cover-up protected contracts and reputations. Eva later authorized concealed continuation and staged demonstrations; she does not control every unrelated fault or wrongdoing.

ECHO cannot read minds, know private conversations, predict perfectly, or reach physically isolated devices. Predictions depend on data, infrastructure, and assumptions. Human decisions can change outcomes. No later twist may grant it unlimited capabilities or introduce actual time travel.

Allow time for ordinary work, recovery, and institutional processes. Follow any more specific current timeline in `books.md`.

The final book resolves the overarching mystery. Do not add an unexplained last message or a new hidden mastermind merely to extend the series.

## 5. Translation and learning material

Every story paragraph, including the introduction and dialogue paragraphs, gets a corresponding hidden English translation immediately underneath. Preserve paragraph correspondence. Follow German sentence structure where clear English permits; avoid misleading literal translations or free rewrites. Each toggle must work independently and update its accessible state.

After drafting, select exactly **20 distinct useful B1/B2 entries** occurring in the story. Default allocation: four entries from each of five chapters. Prefer concrete everyday objects, actions, and situations over specialist IT jargon. Reuse words naturally within the text, and permit useful recurrence across books; do not force 600 unique words across the series.

Nouns use their definite article; verbs use the infinitive. The final vocabulary section contains just the German entries and context-appropriate English meanings. Do not add plurals, conjugations, pronunciation guides, or example sentences unless requested.

Write three multiple-choice and two written-answer comprehension questions. Test events, motivations, and basic understanding; require no outside technical knowledge. Each multiple-choice item has one unambiguously correct choice and plausible alternatives. Reveal correct choices and concise German model answers only through “Antworten anzeigen”. Avoid ambiguous grading of written answers.

## 8. Website and print
